Andreas Family — Arrival Day

Haneda, Step by Step

Wed Sep 30, 2026 ·HND 8:10 am → Kaihinsou Kamakura

Cash, then Suica, then Kamakura — in that order, one step at a time. Nothing here is timed or booked, so if a step takes twice as long, the day still works.

Part of the Japan trip itinerary. Station names are printed large in kanji where they matter — the full study list is in DESTINATIONS-KANJI.rtf.
0 On the plane, before landing last hour of the flight
  1. Have the Visit Japan Web QR codes ready (immigration + customs) and screenshot them so they work with no wi-fi. If they aren't set up, no problem — paper forms are handed out on board and available in the hall.
  2. Move passport, US cash, and a credit card into one pocket you can reach without opening the suitcase.
  3. Set your watch to Japan time. It will be Wednesday morning when you land, and the plan is to stay awake until about 9 pm.
1 Off the plane to the arrival lobby 8:10 → ~9:30 am
  1. Follow the overhead signs marked Arrival / Immigration. There is only one way to go — keep walking with the crowd.
  2. Immigration: passport plus QR code (or paper form). They take a photo and fingerprints. 20–40 minutes in line is normal.
  3. Baggage claim, then Customs (another QR scan or the paper slip). Free luggage carts are everywhere — take one.
  4. You come out into the Arrival Lobby, 2nd floor, Terminal 3. Stop here. This one room has everything in steps 2 and 3. Find a bench, sit for two minutes, get your bearings.
If anything goes sideways: the Airport Information counters in this lobby are staffed with English speakers and are used to tired, turned-around travelers. Ask. That is what they are there for.
2 Get yen — currency exchange counter ~15 min
  1. In the 2F Arrival Lobby, find the Japan Airport Terminal foreign currency exchange counter. It is in this lobby, generally to your left as you come out of the customs exit, and it is open 24 hours. Signs read "Currency Exchange" or "Foreign Exchange."
  2. Hand over your US dollars, and your passport if asked. They take US bills and coins; elsewhere in Japan, don't count on coins.
  3. Change $400–500 to start. Whatever the rate board says is the rate — there is no negotiating and no better booth hiding elsewhere in the terminal. That is roughly ¥60,000–75,000, which covers taxis, meals and small shops for the first several days.
  4. Ask for small bills — "smaller bills, please." A stack of ¥10,000 notes is awkward; you want ¥1,000 notes for taxis, ticket machines and vending machines.
  5. Put the yen in your wallet before you walk away from the counter, and keep the receipt.
Do not use the "Pocket Change" kiosk for this. That machine is for dumping leftover foreign coins on the way out of Japan, not for buying yen. A 7-Eleven ATM also works and takes US cards, but the staffed counter is the simpler move when you are holding cash.
3 Buy and load a Welcome Suica ~15–30 min
  1. From the same lobby, follow signs for Trains / Tokyo Monorail 東京モノレール / Keikyu Line 京急線. The Welcome Suica machines sit by the Tokyo Monorail ticket area and the JR East Travel Service Center in Terminal 3. They are dedicated machines labeled "Welcome Suica," not the ordinary ticket machines.
  2. Touch the screen and pick English first — every one of these machines has it.
  3. Choose Welcome Suica and load ¥10,000 (the machine offers set amounts from ¥1,000 to ¥10,000). There is no deposit — every yen goes onto the card. The airport machines are the one place you can pay by credit card; after that, top-ups are cash.
  4. Take the card and the receipt. Put the card somewhere you can reach in one motion — a shirt pocket, not the bottom of a bag. You will tap it at every gate, several times a day.
What to know about the card
  1. Good for 28 days from the first tap — the whole trip is 16 days, so it will outlast you.
  2. Balance cap is ¥20,000. Any leftover balance is forfeited at expiry and cannot be refunded, so keep it modest and top up as you go.
  3. Works on nearly every train, subway and bus on this trip — JR, Keikyu, Enoden, and the Kyoto, Osaka and Kanazawa systems — and pays at convenience stores and many restaurants. It does not pay Shinkansen fares; those are separate tickets.
  4. Top up at any station ticket machine (there is an English button) or at a 7-Eleven, FamilyMart or Lawson register — hand the clerk the card and cash and say "charge, please."
If the line is long or the machines are down: two clean fallbacks. (a) Buy a TOURIST PASMO at the Keikyu Tourist Information Center in Terminal 3 — same idea, works everywhere Suica does. (b) Skip the card for now and buy a paper ticket to Kamakura at the Keikyu machine; you can get an IC card any day later. Don't stand in a 45-minute queue while jet-lagged.
4 Train to Kamakura — Keikyu to Yokohama, then JR ~80 min riding, ~¥800
  1. Follow signs for the Keikyu Line 京急線 (sometimes written "Keikyu Airport Line") down from the arrival lobby to the ticket gates. Tap the Suica flat on the gate reader — look for the ticket gate sign, 改札. The panel turns green and the gate stays open. Don't insert it anywhere.
  2. Board a train toward Yokohama 横浜. Check the destination on the platform board and on the front of the train — those two characters are the ones that matter.
  3. Ride roughly 25–30 minutes to Yokohama 横浜. Stops are announced and displayed in English. Yokohama is a big, unmistakable station and most of the train gets off there.
  4. At Yokohama, tap out of the Keikyu gates, follow signs for JR Lines — a 2–3 minute walk on the same level — and tap in at the JR gates.
  5. Take the JR Yokosuka Line 横須賀線 toward Zushi 逗子 or Kurihama 久里浜 (a Shonan-Shinjuku Line 湘南新宿ライン train toward Zushi works too). About 25–30 minutes; Kamakura is one stop past Ofuna 大船 — when you see those, start gathering your bags. Get off at Kamakura 鎌倉 and tap out.

The one trap: many Keikyu trains out of Haneda run the other way, to Shinagawa 品川 and central Tokyo. You want one whose route shows Yokohama 横浜 — destinations like Yokohama, Kanazawa-Bunko, Zushi-Hayama or Misakiguchi. If you end up on a Shinagawa train, get off at Keikyu Kamata 京急蒲田 a few minutes along and cross the platform to a Yokohama-bound train. No harm done and nothing extra on the Suica. Staff at the gate will point you to the right platform if you just say "Yokohama?"
The old direct airport bus to Kamakura is suspended (since March 2024) — don't go looking for it. A taxi the whole way is possible but runs well over ¥20,000.
5 Kamakura Station to Kaihinsou ~5–10 min
  1. Easiest with luggage — taxi タクシー. Walk out the East Exit (the way out is 出口) of Kamakura Station 鎌倉 to the taxi rank and show the driver the card below. About 5–8 minutes, roughly ¥1,000–1,500. The rear door opens and closes by itself — stand back and let it. Many taxis take Suica or cards; have cash ready anyway.
  2. Or the little train. The Enoden 江ノ電 (Enoshima Electric Railway) platform is inside the west side of Kamakura Station. Ride 2 stops to Yuigahama 由比ガ浜 — about 4 minutes, ~¥200 on the Suica — then a 1 minute walk to the hotel.
  3. Walking from Kamakura Station is about 20 minutes. Fine another day; not today, not with bags.

6 At the hotel — and staying upright ~noon onward
  1. Realistically you arrive 11:30 am – 12:30 pm, well before afternoon check-in. That is fine — leave the luggage at the front desk and go get lunch. Passport out; they will want it at check-in.
  2. Do not nap. Eat something, walk to the beach three minutes away, get daylight on your face. Bed around 9 pm and the jet lag is mostly settled by Thursday.
  3. The big baths run 3:00–11:30 pm and again 6:30–8:30 am. An evening soak is the best thing going for a body that has been in a seat for twelve hours.
  4. Melissa lands NRT 3:30 pm, and Narita is roughly 2–2.5 hours out to Kamakura, so expect her at the hotel around 6–7 pm. Plan dinner late and easy.
Hard deadline: the hotel cannot check anyone in after 9:00 pm. Nothing about this day should come near that, but if it goes badly, call 0467-22-0960 rather than just turning up late.
